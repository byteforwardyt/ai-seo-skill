# Social and video search

Use for TikTok, Instagram, Pinterest, LinkedIn, and YouTube. These are separate search engines. A Google title tag does not rank a video.

## Shared rule

Optimize the text and the picture a person sees on that platform. Do not export a blog post and call it a video strategy. Confirm the audience searches there before recommending a channel. Audience data beats a generation-wide statistic.

## YouTube

YouTube search and Google video results both read the public video metadata.

- Title: what the video answers, specific enough to choose. A keyword list is a bad title here too.
- Description: the first lines are the snippet. Say what the video covers, then add links and chapters.
- Chapters: real timestamps in the description, matching real topic changes. Fake chapters that do not match the video are a defect.
- Captions: upload an accurate file if the auto-captions are wrong. Names, products, and steps are where auto-captions fail.
- Thumbnail: readable at small size, truthful about the video. Do not promise a result the video does not show.
- Playlist and end screen help a person watch the next relevant video. They are navigation, not a ranking hack.
- The watch page URL can also be a Google result. A unique title and a description that matches the spoken content help both surfaces.

Measure in YouTube Analytics: impressions, click-through rate, average view duration, and the traffic source. Do not invent a rank tracker for YouTube and treat it as ground truth.

## TikTok and Instagram

Search uses the words spoken, the words on screen, the caption, the account name, and how people respond.

- Say the subject in the first spoken line and in on-screen text if the video depends on being found.
- Caption: the query a person would type, plus the context they need. Hashtags are a label, not a paragraph of repeats.
- Cover frame: readable and honest.
- Do not hide the keyword only in a hashtag and never say it.
- Reels and TikToks are not interchangeable uploads. Check the native crop, length, and cover before assuming one file serves both.

Measure saves, shares, and profile visits in the native analytics. Those are closer to "this was useful" than a vanity view count.

## Pinterest

Pinterest search is closer to a visual catalog.

- Pin title and description should name the object and the use.
- The image should show the thing, with text only if it stays readable as a small pin.
- Board names should be categories a searcher understands, not internal campaign names.
- A pin that links to a thin or broken page wastes the click. Fix the landing page.

## LinkedIn

LinkedIn search finds people, companies, and posts. It is not a substitute for Google.

- Profile headline and about section should say what the person does in the words a buyer uses.
- Company page: the real name, specialties, and a description that matches the site.
- Posts are found by the people in the network and by search inside LinkedIn. Do not expect a LinkedIn post to rank in Google unless it is public and worth indexing.

## What not to do

Do not recommend a hashtag block of thirty repeats. Do not recommend engagement pods. Do not claim a percentage of a generation uses a platform as a search engine unless the user brought a sourced study, and even then label the source and the year.
